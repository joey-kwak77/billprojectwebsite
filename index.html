Background information

Transportation policy affects both the environment and everyday budgets.

The bill responds to a national challenge with a policy that is ambitious enough to matter and flexible enough to be implemented.

Transportation is a major source of greenhouse gas emissions in the United States. Cars, trucks, and other vehicles burn petroleum-based fuels, creating emissions that affect air quality and contribute to climate change.

The federal government already regulates vehicle fuel economy through programs such as Corporate Average Fuel Economy standards. The Fuel-Efficient Vehicle Incentive Act builds on that foundation by pairing gradual standards with financial support for consumers and manufacturers.

Instead of requiring Americans to buy one particular type of vehicle, the proposal rewards efficiency, encourages innovation, and gives manufacturers time to develop affordable technologies.

Why this approach?

A transition works best when it reduces pollution while recognizing the different needs of families, workers, rural communities, and American manufacturers.

Fuel-Efficient Vehicle Incentive Act

A Bill to establish incentives and standards for fuel-efficient vehicles in the United States

Be it enacted by the House of Representatives and the United States Senate in Congress assembled, that:
Purpose & Goals: Recognizing that transportation is a major source of air pollution and greenhouse gas emissions in the United States, this bill seeks to reduce fuel consumption by encouraging the production and purchase of more fuel-efficient vehicles. Rather than banning gasoline-powered vehicles, this Act will use incentives and reasonable efficiency standards to gradually improve the fuel economy of vehicles sold in the United States.
Section 1

Short Title

This Act may be cited as the “Fuel-Efficient Vehicle Incentive Act.”

Section 2

Fuel-Efficiency Standards

(a) The Environmental Protection Agency and National Highway Traffic Safety Administration shall gradually increase fuel-efficiency standards for new passenger vehicles.

(b) Standards shall be reviewed regularly to account for improvements in technology, affordability, and fuel efficiency.

(c) Automobile manufacturers shall be given reasonable time to meet new standards.

Section 3

Consumer Incentives

(a) The federal government shall provide a tax credit of up to $3,000 for individuals purchasing qualifying fuel-efficient vehicles.

(b) Vehicles that significantly exceed federal efficiency standards shall qualify for larger credits.

(c) The Department of Energy and Environmental Protection Agency shall determine which vehicles qualify.

Section 4

Support for American Manufacturers

(a) Automobile manufacturers producing vehicles in the United States may receive federal grants for developing technology that improves fuel efficiency.

(b) Eligible improvements may include more efficient engines, hybrid systems, lighter materials, improved aerodynamics, and other technologies that reduce fuel consumption.

(c) Priority shall be given to projects that develop or manufacture these technologies within the United States.

Section 5

Consumer Choice

(a) Nothing in this Act shall prohibit the purchase or sale of gasoline-powered vehicles.

(b) This Act shall not establish a federal ban on gasoline-powered vehicles.

(c) Consumers shall remain free to choose the type of vehicle that they acquire and use.

Section 6

Funding and Evaluation

(a) Congress shall authorize $2 billion annually for five years to fund the programs established under this Act.

(b) The Environmental Protection Agency and Department of Transportation shall evaluate the Act’s effects on fuel consumption, emissions, vehicle prices, and consumers.

(c) A report on the effectiveness of the Act shall be submitted to Congress every two years.

Section 7

Effective Date

This Act shall take effect one year after its passage to provide federal agencies and automobile manufacturers sufficient time to prepare for implementation.

Conclusion: The Fuel-Efficient Vehicle Incentive Act will encourage cleaner and more efficient transportation without eliminating consumer choice. By rewarding consumers for purchasing efficient vehicles and helping American manufacturers develop better technology, this Act provides a gradual and practical approach to reducing fuel consumption and transportation-related pollution.

A concise explanation of the problem, the proposed solution, and why the legislation deserves consideration.

Prepared by Joey Kwak · Canyon Crest Academy

November 1, 2026
The Honorable Scott Peters
United States House of Representatives

Subject: Support for the Fuel-Efficient Vehicle Incentive Act

Dear Representative Peters,

My name is Joey Kwak, and I am a student at Canyon Crest Academy. I am writing to ask for your support in considering the Fuel-Efficient Vehicle Incentive Act, a proposal designed to reduce fuel consumption and transportation-related pollution while preserving consumer choice.

Transportation is a major source of greenhouse gas emissions in the United States, and cars and trucks remain an important part of daily life for families, workers, and businesses. Improving vehicle efficiency can lower the amount of fuel people use, reduce pollution, and help households spend less on gasoline over time. However, a successful transition should recognize that consumers and communities have different needs.

My bill takes a gradual approach. It directs the Environmental Protection Agency and National Highway Traffic Safety Administration to strengthen fuel-efficiency standards over time, while requiring regular reviews of technology and affordability. It also provides a tax credit of up to $3,000 for people who purchase qualifying fuel-efficient vehicles and offers grants to American manufacturers developing more efficient engines, hybrid systems, lighter materials, and other technologies.

Most importantly, the Act does not ban gasoline-powered vehicles. Consumers would remain free to choose the type of vehicle that best fits their needs. By combining reasonable standards with incentives and support for American manufacturing, this legislation encourages progress without making the transition unnecessarily disruptive.

I hope you will consider supporting this proposal and the goal of making transportation cleaner, more efficient, and more affordable. Thank you for your time and for representing our community.

Sincerely,

Joey KwakCanyon Crest Academy
